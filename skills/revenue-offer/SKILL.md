# Smallest Credible Offer

Create something the user can discuss or sell before building infrastructure.

## Evidence first

Identify:

- a reachable buyer;
- a costly, urgent, frequent, or emotionally significant problem;
- proof the user can responsibly claim;
- the smallest useful outcome deliverable with current capability;
- constraints that bound scope and risk.

Label buyer pain, willingness to pay, price, and conversion expectations as hypotheses unless directly evidenced.

## Offer structure

Draft:

1. For whom: a specific reachable buyer, not “everyone.”
2. Trigger: the situation that makes the problem salient now.
3. Outcome: a truthful, bounded result or deliverable.
4. Method: enough to create trust, without inventing a proprietary mechanism.
5. Scope: included, excluded, client responsibilities, and timeline.
6. Proof: only verified examples, experience, artifacts, or transparent pilot status.
7. Price hypothesis: a number or range with reasoning and what would change it.
8. Risk boundary: revision limit, pilot scope, milestone, or refund term the user can actually honor.
9. Next step: a low-friction conversation, diagnostic, or paid commitment.

If confidence is low, reduce scope or claims—not price automatically.

## Validation plan

Choose the smallest respectful test:

- discuss with three relevant people;
- send a small batch of personalized messages;
- offer a paid pilot to a warm lead;
- present the offer after a diagnostic conversation;
- request a deposit before building custom delivery infrastructure.

Do not recommend a website, app, course platform, sales funnel, or automation until conversations or paid evidence demonstrate the need.

## Output

Provide:

- one-sentence offer;
- concise scope and price hypothesis;
- proof and uncertainty labels;
- the three strongest objections with honest responses;
- a validation message or conversation opener;
- one 15–45 minute next action.

Do not publish, send, or commit to final commercial terms without explicit confirmation.
