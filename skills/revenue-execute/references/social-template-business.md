# Social Media Template Business Playbook

Use this playbook only when the active experiment involves selling social media templates or offering campaign design. Adapt the work split; do not assume every user needs end-to-end production.

## Workstreams to calibrate

- niche and buyer research;
- offer and product positioning;
- campaign concept;
- visual direction and design system;
- design production and editable-file setup;
- copywriting, captions, and calls to action;
- mockups and portfolio presentation;
- marketplace listing and keywords;
- promotion, outreach, delivery, and iteration.

## Common collaboration shapes

### Designer who needs commercial and copy support

Keep final visual production user-owned. The agent may research the niche, write the brief, generate concept directions, supply raw copy, prepare listing language, and critique the design. Do not force a design integration.

### Strategist who needs design implementation

Keep audience, objective, positioning, and message user-led. Let the agent produce editable design variants when a suitable tool is available. Approve the visual system and first three representative assets before batching.

### Concept owner who wants end-to-end execution

Let the agent research, position, write, design, create mockups, and draft the listing within agreed boundaries. Require a POC gate before full production and preserve user approval over claims, brand direction, pricing, and publication.

### Beginner who wants to learn

Explain key decisions, provide a small exercise or editable draft, critique the user's attempt, and gradually reduce scaffolding. Do not optimize away the learning objective.

## Minimum credible POC

A useful sample pack may contain:

- one concise buyer and brand brief;
- one visual system with type, color, spacing, and image direction;
- three to five feed designs that demonstrate variation;
- one carousel or story sequence when relevant;
- caption and CTA examples;
- one product mockup;
- a draft product title, description, and positioning hypothesis.

Keep the POC small enough to revise. Label it as a concept or sample unless it represents verified client work. Never invent a client, testimonial, campaign result, sales count, or performance metric.

## Production and tool choices

- If editable templates are part of the promise, validate editability before visual polish.
- Match the production path to the marketplace's actual delivery format and licensing rules.
- Use Canva, Figma, native SVG, image generation, or another tool only after verifying availability and fit.
- Test one export and one duplicate/edit workflow before batching.
- Keep text, image, font, and asset licensing traceable.

## Market test

Choose one small test: show the POC to relevant buyers, publish a clearly labeled sample after confirmation, offer a paid custom pilot, or test one listing. Record conversations, saves or clicks only as early signals; paid commitment and delivered value remain stronger evidence.
