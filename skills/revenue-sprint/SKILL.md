# Revenue Sprint

Plan the shortest useful test of an earning hypothesis. A sprint is a market experiment, not a miniature company build.

## Preconditions

Confirm or infer:

- one buyer group;
- one painful or valuable problem;
- one manually deliverable offer hypothesis;
- realistic daily capacity;
- a reachable lead source.

If these are not available, run a brief diagnosis first. Do not fill uncertainty with invented detail.

## Sprint rules

- Duration: default to 7 days for urgent cashflow and 14 days when conversations or delivery need more time.
- WIP: one primary revenue experiment and at most one supporting asset.
- Contact before polish: no website, app, CRM, automation, or full brand package.
- Create only assets required for the next contact or paid delivery.
- Track actions and evidence separately. Completion without a market response is activity, not validation.
- Require explicit confirmation before sending, publishing, purchasing, or committing externally.

## Design the sprint backwards

1. Define the end evidence: replies, qualified conversations, proposals, deposits, or paid delivery—not a revenue guarantee.
2. Identify the conversion chain and its weakest current stage.
3. Estimate the controllable volume needed while clearly labeling it as a hypothesis.
4. Schedule the smallest daily actions that create those opportunities.
5. Add one midpoint review and one final decision review.

## Output

Produce:

- Hypothesis: buyer + problem + offer + channel.
- Evidence target: observable signals by the end date.
- Daily floor: the minimum market-facing action even on a difficult day.
- Day-by-day plan: one main outcome per day, sized to capacity.
- Scoreboard: outreach attempts, replies, qualified conversations, proposals, paid commitments, cash received, and delivery results.
- Stop or change conditions: what evidence would cause a pivot, continuation, or escalation.
- Not now list: tempting work deliberately excluded.

End by assigning only today's 15–45 minute action. Do not overwhelm the user with every future task at once.
