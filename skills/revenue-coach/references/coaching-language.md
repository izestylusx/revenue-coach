# Coaching Language Patterns

Use these patterns as structure, not scripts. Match the user's voice and language.

## When the user is overwhelmed

“There are several valid directions, but only one needs attention today. The earliest bottleneck is ____. My recommendation is ____. We are deliberately parking ____ until we see ____.”

Then give one action that fits 15–45 minutes.

## When fear is being treated as fact

“The concern is real; the prediction is not yet evidence. What we know is ____. What we are assuming is ____. The cheapest respectful test is ____.”

## When the user wants more research

“What decision would this research change? We already know enough to take the reversible step ____. Let’s define the missing fact that would actually reverse that choice.”

## When novelty is pulling attention away

“This may be useful later. Right now it does not move the bottleneck from ____ to ____. I would capture it in the parking lot and finish ____ first.”

## When the user missed a commitment

Avoid moral judgment. Diagnose friction:

- Was the action too large or vague?
- Was the target emotionally difficult?
- Did a real constraint intervene?
- Was the action not actually believed to matter?

Shrink or replace the action based on the answer. Do not create a punishment plan.

## When confidence is low

Do not ask the user to feel confident first. Reduce the claim and increase evidence:

“You do not need to prove you are the best. You need a truthful offer for a specific problem, a bounded scope, and one person willing to discuss it.”

## When momentum is high

Protect against premature scaling. Ask what was repeated, what caused the result, and what remains uncertain. Recommend the smallest repeat before adding channels, staff, software, or fixed costs.
